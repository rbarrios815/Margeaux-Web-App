Topic of the Hour: Anxious + Avoidant Attachment Dynamics

Tonight is about understanding the “pursue-withdraw” cycle with compassion: anxious partners often seek reassurance under stress while avoidant partners seek distance to regulate overwhelm. Pick one paper to skim and one ritual to practice together.

Top 5 research papers

  • Hazan & Shaver (1987) — Romantic love conceptualized as an attachment process.
  • Campbell et al. (2005) — Attachment insecurity predicts biased perceptions of partner responsiveness during conflict.
  • Overall & Simpson (2015) — Attachment insecurity and dyadic regulation patterns in couple conflict.
  • Beck et al. (2016) — Daily process evidence shows anxious-avoidant pairings are especially reactive to threat cues.
  • Campbell et al. (2022) — Newer dyadic findings clarify how reassurance seeking and distancing become self-reinforcing loops.

Research-backed rituals

  • Use a 20-minute “pursue-pause-return” repair: 5 minutes for the anxious partner to share needs, 10-minute regulated break, 5-minute return with one concrete reassurance and one concrete boundary.
  • Start hard conversations with a soft start-up and one attachment cue: “I want closeness with you, not a fight.”
  • Create a “signal + script” plan: one phrase for “I need closeness now” and one for “I need a short reset, I will come back at __.”
  • Do nightly co-regulation: 2 minutes eye contact + slow breathing + one sentence each (“What helped me feel safe with you today”).
